What Is a Prop Firm Challenge? A Complete Beginner's Guide

Now Funded Team · Prop Trading 101

If you've ever wondered how traders get access to six-figure accounts without risking their own savings, the answer almost always involves a prop firm challenge. It's the entry point into proprietary (prop) trading, and it's designed to answer one simple question for the firm: can this trader manage risk consistently enough to be trusted with real capital?

This guide walks through exactly what a challenge is, how it's structured, what typically gets you disqualified, and what happens on the other side once you pass.

The Basic Idea

A prop firm challenge is a simulated trading evaluation. You pay a one-time fee for access to a demo account of a chosen size — say ₦200,000 or ₦1,000,000 — and you're given a set of rules to trade within. If you hit a profit target while respecting the rules, you "pass" the challenge and move on to a funded account, where you trade with the firm's capital and keep a share of the profits.

The fee isn't really "buying an account" in the traditional sense — it's buying an attempt at the evaluation. Firms make challenges self-sustaining this way, which is what lets them offer accounts far larger than most retail traders could otherwise access.

What You're Actually Being Evaluated On

Every firm's exact numbers differ, but the rules almost always cover the same core areas:

  • Profit target — the percentage gain you need to reach to pass (commonly 8–10% per phase).
  • Maximum daily drawdown — how much your account balance or equity is allowed to drop in a single day before you're disqualified.
  • Maximum overall drawdown — the total drawdown allowed from your starting balance across the entire challenge.
  • Minimum trading days — a floor on how many separate days you must trade, to discourage one lucky session from "passing" the evaluation.
  • Consistency rules (on some plans) — limits on how much of your total profit can come from a single trade or day, to filter out lucky one-off gambles.
Why these specific rules? Prop firms aren't trying to trip you up — they're trying to simulate the discipline required to manage real capital responsibly. A trader who can hit a target while respecting a daily drawdown limit has demonstrated something a lucky single trade never could: repeatable risk control.

Single-Step vs Multi-Step Challenges

Most firms offer either a 1-step, 2-step, or occasionally a 3-step path to funding. A 1-step challenge has one profit target to clear before you're funded. A 2-step challenge splits that into a first phase (usually a higher target, like 10%) and a second phase (a lower confirmation target, like 5%), giving the firm two separate checkpoints on your consistency before handing over real capital. Fewer steps generally means a higher fee for the same account size, since the firm is taking on more risk with less evidence of your discipline.

What Happens When You Pass

Once you clear all phases, you're moved to a funded account — typically the same size as your challenge account, sometimes with a scaling plan that grows it further based on continued performance. From there, the drawdown rules usually still apply (to protect the firm's capital), but the profit target requirement disappears — you're simply trading and requesting payouts on your share of the profit, commonly split somewhere between 70/30 and 90/10 in the trader's favor.

Common Reasons Traders Fail

  • Oversizing positions to hit the profit target faster, which makes the daily drawdown limit far easier to breach.
  • Not knowing the exact daily reset time for the drawdown calculation, and getting caught by an open position rolling into a new day.
  • Treating the challenge fee as "spare money" and revenge-trading after a loss instead of respecting the plan.
  • Ignoring minimum trading day requirements and rushing the target in one or two sessions.
