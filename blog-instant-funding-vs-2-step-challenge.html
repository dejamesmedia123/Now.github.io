Instant Funding vs 2-Step Challenge: Which Should You Choose?

Now Funded Team · Choosing a Plan

Nearly every prop firm now offers more than one path to a funded account, and the choice usually comes down to two philosophies: pay more up front to skip the evaluation entirely (instant funding), or pay less and prove yourself over two phases first (a 2-step challenge). Neither is objectively "better" — they suit different traders and different risk tolerances.

How Instant Funding Works

With instant funding, there's no evaluation phase at all — you pay a fee and are placed directly onto a funded account (often run through the firm's internal simulated book initially, same as any funded account). There's no profit target to hit and no minimum trading days to satisfy before you can request a payout. The tradeoff is a meaningfully higher fee for the same account size, since the firm is taking on the account without any evidence of your trading discipline.

How a 2-Step Challenge Works

A 2-step challenge splits the evaluation into two checkpoints: a first phase (commonly a 10% profit target) and a second, usually easier confirmation phase (commonly 5%), both bound by the same daily and overall drawdown limits. Only after clearing both do you receive the funded account. The fee is lower because the firm gets two rounds of evidence that your risk management is repeatable before any capital-equivalent account is on the line.

The core tradeoff in one line: instant funding buys you time; a 2-step challenge buys you a lower price. Neither changes the drawdown rules you'll trade under once funded.

Which One Should You Actually Pick?

  • Pick instant funding if you've already got a proven track record (even on a personal demo or live account), you value getting to payout-eligible status quickly, and the higher fee is a smaller factor than time.
  • Pick a 2-step challenge if you're newer to funded trading, want a cheaper way to test whether a given firm's rules and platform suit you, or simply prefer proving consistency over two checkpoints before capital is at stake.

Whichever you choose, the drawdown discipline is identical either way — see our breakdown of daily vs maximum drawdown to make sure you understand exactly what you'll be trading under from day one.
