Daily Drawdown vs Maximum Drawdown: What's the Difference?

Now Funded Team · Rules & Risk Management

Ask any experienced funded trader what actually gets people disqualified, and drawdown rules — not missed profit targets — will usually top the list. The frustrating part is that most breaches aren't caused by reckless trading; they're caused by traders not fully understanding how these two limits are calculated. Let's fix that.

Maximum Daily Drawdown

This is the maximum your account is allowed to drop in a single trading day, measured from a fixed reference point set at the start of that day — usually the previous day's closing balance or equity, whichever the firm's rules specify. If your account falls below that threshold at any point during the day, even briefly on an open trade, it's typically an instant breach.

For example, on a ₦200,000 account with a 5% daily drawdown limit, your equity cannot drop below ₦190,000 at any point that day relative to your starting reference for that day. It doesn't matter if you recover before the day ends — many firms flag the breach the moment the floor is crossed, not at day's end.

The most common accidental breach: holding an open position overnight into a new trading day. If that position is floating at a loss when the new day's reference balance is set, and then moves further against you, you can breach a limit you thought you had room under — because the calculation reset around your open trade, not the other way around.

Maximum Overall Drawdown

This is the total drawdown allowed across the entire evaluation or funded account, measured from your very first starting balance (not a daily rolling figure). Some firms use a "static" maximum drawdown — a fixed floor that never moves regardless of profit — while others use a "trailing" drawdown that rises along with your peak balance, effectively locking in some of your gains as a new floor.

TypeHow It Behaves
Static Maximum DrawdownFixed floor set at the start — e.g. never below 90% of your original ₦200,000 starting balance, regardless of how high your balance climbs.
Trailing Maximum DrawdownFloor rises with your highest-ever balance — if you grow the account to ₦220,000, the floor recalculates from that new peak, not the original balance.

Why the Distinction Matters So Much

Confusing the two rules leads to two very different — and very common — mistakes:

  • Traders who forget the daily limit resets each day sometimes assume they have more room left than they actually do, because they're mentally tracking only the overall limit.
  • Traders on a trailing maximum drawdown sometimes get caught off guard when their "cushion" shrinks after a strong winning streak, because the floor moved up with their peak balance.

How to Actually Stay Inside Both Limits

  1. Know your exact daily reset time and time zone before you place a single trade.
  2. Size positions so that a single stop-loss, even a doubled or tripled bad day, can't approach your daily limit.
  3. Avoid holding meaningfully sized positions across the daily reset unless you've deliberately accounted for the worst-case move against you.
  4. If your account uses a trailing maximum drawdown, recalculate your real cushion after every new equity high — don't trade off yesterday's number.
