MT5 Funded Accounts Explained: How Funded Trading Actually Works

Now Funded Team · Prop Trading 101

Once you pass a prop firm challenge, you're handed something called a "funded MT5 account." It sounds simple, but a lot of new traders aren't totally sure what's actually happening behind the scenes — is it real money? Whose money? What happens if you lose it? This guide clears all of that up.

What "Funded" Actually Means

A funded account is a live (or live-simulated, depending on the firm's internal book) MetaTrader 5 account where you trade under the firm's capital, not your own. You get a login, an investor password, and a server address just like any other MT5 account — the difference is what happens to the profit and loss.

Behind the scenes, most firms run funded accounts through a staged internal process often called a "book stage." Early on, your trades typically run in a simulated (B-Book) environment — the firm isn't yet risking real capital on your specific trades, but every rule, target, and drawdown limit applies exactly as if it were real. As your track record proves consistent, some firms begin blending in live-market execution (a hybrid or "H-Book" stage), and top performers may eventually be invited to trade the firm's real capital directly as an in-house trader, sometimes with a salary on top of the profit split.

Why does this staging exist? It protects the firm's actual capital from being risked on unproven trading behavior, while still giving you a completely real trading experience — the same rules, the same numbers, the same consequences for breaching drawdown.

Balance vs Equity vs Starting Balance

Three numbers matter on a funded account, and mixing them up is one of the most common sources of confusion:

TermWhat It Means
Starting BalanceThe account size you were funded at — this is the number your profit split and drawdown limits are measured against.
Current BalanceYour balance after all closed trades — doesn't move until a trade is closed.
EquityYour current balance adjusted for any open trades' floating profit or loss in real time.

Drawdown rules are usually calculated off whichever of balance or equity is lower at any given moment — which is exactly why an open losing trade can trigger a breach even if you haven't closed it yet.

How the Profit Split Works

Once funded, your profit target requirement disappears — there's no more "hitting 10%" to worry about. Instead, you simply trade, and periodically request a payout on the profits you've generated. The firm keeps a percentage (the "split"), and you keep the rest — commonly somewhere between 70% and 90% in the trader's favor, depending on the plan and how far you've progressed through the firm's stages.

Payouts aren't usually instant on request — most firms review them to confirm the profit is real, sustainable, and wasn't generated through rule violations. If you want the specifics on timing and requirements, see our guide on how prop firm payouts actually work.

What Can Get a Funded Account Closed

  • Breaching the maximum daily or overall drawdown limit — same rules that applied during the challenge usually still apply.
  • Extended account inactivity, on some plans.
  • Rule violations like unauthorized EA (expert advisor) use, where prohibited, or trading around major news events, on plans that restrict it.

If your account is closed for a violation, most firms offer a way to purchase a fresh challenge attempt rather than losing access permanently — always confirm this with whichever firm you're funded through.
