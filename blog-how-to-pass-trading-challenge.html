7 Practical Tips to Pass Your Trading Challenge on the First Try

Now Funded Team · Strategy & Discipline

Here's something most traders don't want to hear: the strategy you already have is probably good enough to pass a challenge. What actually separates traders who pass from traders who don't isn't a secret indicator — it's a handful of boring, repeatable habits around risk. These seven are the ones that matter most.

1 Risk a fixed, small percentage per trade

Before you even think about profit targets, decide your risk-per-trade as a fixed percentage — commonly 0.5–1% of account balance — and never deviate from it regardless of how confident a setup feels. This single habit does more to prevent a drawdown breach than any entry strategy ever will.

2 Know your daily drawdown reset time cold

Every firm calculates the daily drawdown from a specific reference point at a specific time, usually tied to server time. If you don't know exactly when your trading day "resets," you're trading half-blind. Write it down, set a reminder, and treat it as seriously as your stop-loss levels. For the full breakdown of how this rule actually works, see our guide on daily vs maximum drawdown.

3 Don't rush the profit target

Trying to hit an 8–10% target in two or three trades almost always means oversizing, which is exactly what daily drawdown limits are designed to catch. Spreading your target across your full minimum trading day requirement (or beyond) keeps your position sizes sane and sustainable.

4 Treat the challenge fee as already spent

The moment you start trading as if you "need" to win back the fee, you'll start taking worse trades to chase it. Mentally write the fee off before you begin — it removes an entire category of bad decision-making tied to loss aversion.

5 Avoid trading straight into high-impact news

Spreads widen and slippage spikes around major economic releases, which can turn a normal-sized stop-loss into a drawdown-breaching one in seconds. Even on plans that don't explicitly restrict news trading, it's one of the highest-variance windows you can choose to trade through.

A simple test: if you wouldn't be comfortable explaining a specific trade's position size to a risk manager who controls real capital, it's probably oversized for a challenge account too.

6 Journal every trade, even the winners

A simple log of entry reason, size, and outcome makes it obvious — fast — when you're drifting from your plan under pressure. Most rule breaches aren't a single bad decision; they're a slow drift over several trades that a journal would have caught early.

7 Plan for the account you'll have after you pass

The habits that get you through a challenge are the same habits that keep a funded account alive long-term. Traders who "switch off" discipline the moment they pass often breach the same drawdown rules on the funded account within weeks. Treat the challenge as a rehearsal for exactly how you intend to trade afterward — because it is one.
