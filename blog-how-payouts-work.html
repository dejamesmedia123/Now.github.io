How Prop Firm Payouts Actually Work: Profit Split, Timing & Requirements

Now Funded Team · Payouts & Withdrawals

Passing a challenge and getting funded is only half the story — the part that actually matters is getting paid. Payout mechanics vary firm to firm, but the underlying logic is similar almost everywhere. Here's what to expect.

When You Become Eligible for Your First Payout

Most firms require a minimum number of active trading days on the funded account, and often a minimum profit amount, before your very first payout request. This exists to confirm the profit is the result of a repeatable process rather than a single lucky trade right after funding. Subsequent payout cycles are usually shorter — commonly every 1–2 weeks once you're established — but always confirm the specific cadence with whichever firm you're funded through.

How the Profit Split Is Calculated

Your split is applied to the profit generated since your last payout (or since funding, for your first request) — not to your total account balance. If your split is 80/20 in your favor and you've generated ₦100,000 in new profit, you'd be eligible to request ₦80,000, with the account retaining the remainder plus your original starting balance.

Splits often improve over time. Many firms increase your share the longer you stay funded without a violation — moving from something like 80/20 toward 90/10 as you build a track record, sometimes alongside progression through internal stages such as B-Book, hybrid, and eventually in-house trader status.

What Can Delay or Reduce a Payout

  • Verification review — firms typically check that profit wasn't generated through prohibited methods (rule violations, unusual arbitrage, exploiting pricing errors) before releasing funds.
  • Incomplete KYC — identity verification is almost always required before your first payout clears, so it's worth completing this the moment you're funded, not when you first request a payout.
  • Missing bank or payment details — have your payout method fully set up in advance so a technical detail doesn't hold up an otherwise-approved request.
  • Suspicious referral patterns — on firms with an affiliate program, payouts tied to flagged self-referral activity are often held for manual review.

A Simple Example

Say you're funded on a ₦500,000 account with an 80/20 split. Over three weeks you grow the account to ₦560,000 — ₦60,000 in new profit. You request a payout: at 80%, you'd be eligible for ₦48,000, with the account's balance mechanics continuing from there for your next cycle.

Getting the Most Out of Your Payout Cycle

  1. Complete KYC immediately after funding — don't wait until you're ready to request your first payout.
  2. Keep your bank or payment details current in your dashboard so nothing stalls at approval time.
  3. Grow the account steadily rather than requesting payouts back-to-back with little growth in between — firms watch this ratio as part of how quickly you progress through their internal stages.
  4. Understand your daily and overall drawdown limits well enough that a payout request never has to compete with avoiding a breach — see our guide on daily vs maximum drawdown for the specifics.
